# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Owners of RICOH GR III, GR IIIx, and GR IV cameras, including the HDF and Monochrome models. Most are photographers, not developers. They come from Reddit, Xiaohongshu, or a friend's photos, on a computer with a card reader. They want the film looks or the framing on their own camera, and they worry about breaking the camera.

## Product Purpose

grkit.app makes a GRKit package for one camera and puts it on the SD card. In Chrome and Edge it writes the card directly: install, update, add or remove modules and effects, and uninstall. Other browsers download a ZIP with an install guide. The effects section lets a person preview each film look on a sample photo or on their own DNG, and import their own LUT.

Success: a first-time user installs GRKit without reading the guide twice, and a returning user updates the card in a few clicks.

## Brand Personality

Precise, quiet, trustworthy. The site should feel like the GR's own menus: square controls, condensed type, black ink on white, and Ricoh red used only as a small accent. Copy is short and plain, for ordinary GR users, with no file names, build IDs, or other technical details.

## Anti-references

- SaaS landing pages: hero metrics, gradient text, feature card grids, testimonials.
- Firmware-mod sites that look risky: dark hacker styling, warning banners everywhere, long technical walls of text.
- Generic "photo app" styling: rounded pastel cards, glassmorphism, film-strip decoration.

## Design Principles

1. One clear next step. At each point, the page shows one primary action.
2. Show before writing. The card changes are listed, and nothing is written until the user confirms.
3. The looks sell themselves. The preview is the emotional peak, so it gets the space, not the decoration.
4. Plain words. Each sentence is something a GR owner can act on.
5. Camera-native. Visual choices follow the GR menu, not web trends.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Visible keyboard focus on all controls. Respect reduced motion. Five languages (English, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Japanese, Korean), so layouts must hold longer and shorter strings.
